How Much House Can I Afford?

Before you fall in love with a listing, know the number that keeps you comfortable — not just what a lender will approve.

Related: How Much Can I Borrow · Mortgage Payment
The rule of thumb
Keep housing under ~28% of income, total debt under ~36%.

That's the 28/36 guideline most lenders reference. It keeps your monthly payment — including taxes and insurance — from swallowing your budget.

The 28/36 rule, explained

28% front-end: your total housing payment (mortgage, taxes, insurance, HOA) should stay under about 28% of your gross monthly income.

36% back-end: all your debts (housing + car + credit cards + student loans) should stay under about 36% of gross income.

What lenders actually look at

  • Income & job stability — your debt-to-income ratio is the big one.
  • Credit score — higher scores unlock lower rates.
  • Down payment — more down = smaller loan, less monthly.
  • Current rates — they move the number a lot.
Get pre-approved first. Lenders give you a real number based on your actual finances, and pre-approval strengthens your offer when you find a home.

Buy below your max

Being approved for a loan doesn't mean you should spend that much. Taxes, insurance, maintenance, and emergencies eat into a budget fast. A common, healthier approach is to buy well below your maximum approval so you have breathing room.

Work through it step by step

  1. Add up your gross monthly income — before taxes, including salary and steady side income.
  2. List your monthly debts — car, cards, student loans, child support.
  3. Estimate your housing payment — mortgage + taxes + insurance + HOA. Use the Mortgage Payment Calculator to get the mortgage part.
  4. Check the two ratios — housing ≤ 28% of income, total debts ≤ 36%.
  5. Back into the price — enter your comfortable payment in the How Much Can I Borrow tool to see the loan size.

Lenders use similar math, but their final number also depends on your credit score and down payment.

Common mistakes

  • Borrowing up to your max approval. Approved ≠ comfortable. Taxes, insurance, and maintenance eat into that number fast.
  • Ignoring your other debts. A car payment you'll keep for 3 more years counts against you now.
  • Forgetting maintenance. Budget roughly 1% of home value per year for upkeep.
  • Quoting your pre-tax income. Your take-home is what actually pays the mortgage — leave real breathing room.

Common questions

What is the 28/36 rule?▾
The 28/36 rule says your housing payment should stay under about 28% of your gross monthly income, and total debt payments under about 36%. It's a common guideline lenders use.
How much house can I afford with a $100k salary?▾
On $100,000 a year, the 28% guideline suggests a housing payment around $2,333 a month, which typically supports a home price in the $400k–$450k range depending on your rate, down payment, and other debts.
What factors do lenders consider?▾
Lenders look at your income, debt-to-income ratio, credit score, down payment, and current interest rates. A higher credit score and lower debt help you qualify for a larger loan at a better rate.
Should I buy more than I'm comfortable with?▾
Lenders approve loans based on ratios, but only you know your real budget. Leave room for taxes, insurance, maintenance, and emergencies. It's usually wiser to buy below your max approval.
