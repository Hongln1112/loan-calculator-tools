How Much Down Payment Do I Need?

The 20% rule is a guide, not a requirement. Here's what you actually need.

Related: How Much Can I Borrow · Mortgage Payment
The short answer
20% avoids PMI — but you can buy with far less.

Conventional loans can go as low as 3–5% down, FHA as low as 3.5%, and some VA/USDA loans at 0%. The trade-off is paying mortgage insurance until you build equity.

Down payment by loan type

Loan typeMinimum downNotes
Conventional3–5%Most flexible; PMI if under 20%

Rates and minimums vary by lender and your credit. These are common starting points, not guarantees.

Why 20% is the sweet spot

  • Avoids PMI — you skip private mortgage insurance, saving each month.
  • Better rate — lenders reward lower loan-to-value with lower rates.
  • More equity — you own more of the home from day one.
But it's not always smart to wait for 20%. If prices are rising or rent keeps climbing, buying now with a smaller down payment can beat waiting years to save more. Weigh your market and timeline.

Bigger isn't always better

A larger down payment shrinks your loan and monthly cost, but it ties up cash you might need for emergencies, repairs, or investments. Many homeowners regret draining their savings.

Down payment vs monthly cost

Here's what different down payments do to a $350,000 home (30-year, 6.5% rate). Smaller down means a bigger loan and higher payment — and PMI until you reach 20% equity:

Down paymentLoan amountMonthly P&IPMI?
20% ($70,000)$280,000≈ $1,770No
10% ($35,000)$315,000≈ $1,991Yes
5% ($17,500)$332,500≈ $2,102Yes
3.5% ($12,250)$337,750≈ $2,135Yes

PMI is usually 0.5–1.5% of the loan per year — a few hundred dollars a month you'll shed once you hit 20% equity.

The trade-off: cash now vs monthly later

Draining savings to hit 20% can backfire. A bigger down payment lowers your payment, but leaves you thin for closing costs, a moving month with double rent, repairs, or a job change. Most pros keep 3–6 months of expenses on top of the down payment.

If you don't yet have that cushion, a smaller down payment with PMI is often the smarter, safer move — you can refinance or wait it out once equity builds.

Where does the down payment come from?

  • Savings — the cleanest source.
  • Gifted funds — family gifts are allowed on many loans, with a documented letter.
  • Retirement accounts — first-time buyers can often use penalty-free withdrawals; check limits.
  • Down-payment assistance programs — many states and cities offer grants or low-interest help.

Lenders need to "source" your funds — large unexplained deposits can slow approval, so keep records.

Common questions

How much down payment do I really need?▾
20% avoids PMI, but many programs allow far less — conventional loans can go as low as 3–5%, and FHA as low as 3.5%. The right amount balances a lower monthly cost against keeping cash for emergencies.
Why does everyone say 20% down?▾
Because 20% is the threshold at which you avoid PMI, and it usually gets you the best rate. But it's not required — putting less down is common, you just pay for insurance until you build 20% equity.
What's the minimum down payment for a house?▾
Conventional loans often start at 3–5%, FHA at 3.5%, and some VA and USDA loans require nothing down. The minimum depends on your loan type, credit, and lender.
Is a bigger down payment always better?▾
Not always. A bigger down payment means a smaller loan and lower monthly cost, but it ties up cash you might need for emergencies, repairs, or investments. Find the balance that keeps you comfortable.
