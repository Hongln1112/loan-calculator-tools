Is It Worth Paying Off My Mortgage Early?

Paying off your mortgage is a guaranteed return — but it may not be your best return.

Related: Payoff Calculator · Interest Calculator
The short answer
It's worth it if your mortgage rate beats what your cash could earn — or you want the peace of mind.

Extra payments save interest and cut years off your loan. But that cash could also be invested. Weigh your rate against your alternatives before deciding.

The case for paying extra

  • Guaranteed savings — every extra dollar reduces interest you'd definitely pay.
  • Shorter loan — you own your home outright years sooner.
  • Peace of mind — a paid-off home removes a big monthly bill and risk.
  • Simpler retirement — lower fixed costs in your later years.

The case for investing instead

  • Higher returns — if you can earn more than your mortgage rate, investing wins.
  • Liquidity — cash is flexible; home equity is tied up.
  • Tax benefits — mortgage interest may be deductible, lowering its real cost.
  • Inflation — a fixed mortgage gets cheaper in real terms over time.
The decision rule: compare your mortgage rate to what you could earn after taxes. If the mortgage costs more than you'd earn, pay it down. If you'd earn more, invest. Use the Payoff Calculator to see the interest you'd save.

Do these first

  • Build an emergency fund — before locking cash into your home.
  • Pay off higher-interest debt — credit cards beat a mortgage in priority.
  • Keep up retirement contributions — don't skip matching contributions to prepay.
  • Check for prepayment penalties — rare on mortgages, but confirm with your lender.

Once those are covered, extra mortgage payments become a strong, safe place for the remaining cash.

Common questions

Is paying off my mortgage early worth it?▾
It saves interest and shortens the loan, but uses cash you could invest. If your mortgage rate is higher than what your money could earn elsewhere — or you value the peace of mind — paying extra makes sense.
Should I pay off my mortgage or invest?▾
Compare your mortgage rate to your expected investment return. If you can earn more after taxes by investing than your mortgage costs, investing may win. If your mortgage rate is high, paying it down is a guaranteed return.
What should I do before paying extra?▾
Build an emergency fund, pay off higher-interest debts first, and make sure you're not neglecting retirement contributions. Then extra mortgage payments are a safe place for the remaining cash.
Does paying extra reduce my monthly payment?▾
Usually not — extra payments shorten the loan term instead of lowering the monthly amount, unless your lender offers recasting. Ask your lender which option applies to you.
