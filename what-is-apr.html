What Is APR?

The number lenders use to make a loan look cheaper — here's what it really means.

Related: What Is a Good Rate · Rate Converter
In one line
APR = the true yearly cost of a loan, including interest and fees.

The interest rate is just the price of borrowing. APR adds the lender fees on top, so it's the fairer number to compare loans.

Why APR matters

Two loans can advertise the same rate but cost different amounts. The one with lower fees has a lower APR. That's why lenders are required to show APR — it lets you compare apples to apples.

When you see a loan offer, look at the APR, not just the headline rate. A slightly higher rate with much lower fees can be the cheaper loan.

What APR doesn't include

  • Appraisal fees and title insurance
  • Prepaid interest and escrow
  • Some closing costs

Ask your lender for a full breakdown of every fee, so there are no surprises at closing.

Common questions

What does APR stand for?▾
APR stands for annual percentage rate. It's the yearly cost of a loan expressed as a percentage, including both the interest rate and certain fees.
What's the difference between APR and the interest rate?▾
The interest rate is just the cost of borrowing the principal. APR adds in lender fees and other costs, so it's usually higher. When comparing loans, the APR is the better number to use.
Is a lower APR always better?▾
Usually yes, for similar loans. But watch the fine print — some loans have low APRs with high origination fees, and a shorter-term loan with a slightly higher APR may still cost less overall.
Does APR include all fees?▾
No. APR includes many lender fees but typically excludes some items like title insurance, appraisal, and prepaid interest. Ask your lender which costs are and aren't included.
