How to use this converter

  • The effective annual rate is the one to compare — it shows the true yearly cost once monthly compounding is factored in.

Why compounding matters

A monthly rate compounds 12 times a year. So a 1% monthly rate is not 12% annual — it's about 12.7% effective, because each month's interest earns interest the next month.

Always compare loans by their effective annual rate or APR, not the quoted nominal rate, so you're comparing like for like.

Common questions

How do I convert a monthly interest rate to annual?▾
A simple estimate multiplies the monthly rate by 12. The accurate method compounds it: (1 + monthly rate)^12 - 1, which gives the effective annual rate.
What's the difference between APR and interest rate?▾
The interest rate is the cost of borrowing only. APR includes fees and other costs, so it's usually higher and gives a truer picture of the loan's annual cost.
Why does compounding matter?▾
The more often interest compounds, the more you actually pay. A 12% annual rate that compounds monthly is effectively more than 12% over a year — this converter shows the real number.
