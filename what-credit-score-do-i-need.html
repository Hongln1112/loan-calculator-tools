What Credit Score Do I Need to Buy a House?

Your score decides both whether you qualify and how much you'll pay.

Related: How Much Can I Afford · What Is a Good Rate
The short answer
Conventional ~620, FHA ~580, and higher scores get better rates.

Minimums vary by loan type. But aiming for 740+ usually unlocks the best rates — and the score you bring decides how much interest you pay over the life of the loan.

Minimums by loan type

Loan typeMin scoreNotes
Conventional~620Most common; 740+ for best rates
FHA~580580 with 3.5% down; 500 with 10%
VA~620For veterans; no set federal minimum
USDA~640Rural; lender sets the bar

Lenders can set higher minimums than these. Confirm with your lender.

How your score shapes your rate

A better score saves real money. The difference between, say, 620 and 740 can lower your rate by half a point or more — which can mean thousands of dollars less in interest over 30 years.

Roughly, scores 740+ get the best rates, 700–740 get good rates, and below 700 the rates climb. Use the Mortgage Payment Calculator to see what a rate difference does to your monthly payment.

How to improve your score

  • Pay down credit cards — utilization is a big factor.
  • Pay every bill on time — payment history matters most.
  • Fix errors — check your reports for mistakes.
  • Avoid new accounts — don't open cards before applying.

Even 20–30 points can lower your rate. If you're not there yet, wait a few months and work on these before applying.

Common questions

What is the minimum credit score for a mortgage?▾
Conventional loans often want at least 620, FHA loans as low as 580 (or 500 with a larger down payment), VA loans typically around 620, and USDA around 640. Higher scores get better rates.
What credit score do I need for the best rate?▾
Scores around 740 or higher usually unlock the most favorable mortgage rates. Between about 700 and 740 you'll still get good rates; below 700, rates rise noticeably.
Can I buy a house with bad credit?▾
Yes, through FHA or VA loans you may qualify with lower scores, but you'll pay higher rates and possibly higher costs. Improving your score before applying can save you significantly.
How can I improve my credit score fast?▾
Pay down credit card balances, pay every bill on time, correct errors on your credit report, and avoid opening new accounts before applying. Raising your score even 20–30 points can lower your rate.
