What Is a Good Mortgage Rate?

There's no single magic number — but here's how to tell if the rate you're being offered is fair.

Related: Mortgage Payment · Rate Converter
Rough benchmark
30-year fixed: roughly 6–7%; 15-year: ~0.5–0.75% lower.

Your actual rate depends on credit, down payment, loan type, and the market. Compare quotes from several lenders — that's the only way to know what's good for you.

What shapes your rate

  • Credit score — the single biggest factor. Higher scores get better rates.
  • Down payment — more down means lower risk to the lender, so a lower rate.
  • Loan type — 30-year fixed vs 15-year vs adjustable all price differently.
  • Loan size & property — some loans cost more to service.
  • Market rates — they move with the economy and Federal Reserve.

How to get a better rate

  • Shop multiple lenders — rates vary, and quotes are free.
  • Boost your credit — pay down cards, check for errors.
  • Larger down payment — lowers risk and rate.
  • Consider a shorter term — 15-year rates are usually lower.
  • Buy points — pay upfront to lower the rate, if you'll stay long enough.
Get 3+ quotes before deciding. Even with identical profiles, lenders can quote meaningfully different rates. Pre-approval from a few gives you leverage.

Fixed vs adjustable, at a glance

  • Fixed-rate — your rate stays the same for the whole term. Predictable, safe, and what most buyers choose.
  • Adjustable (ARM) — a lower rate for an initial period (often 5, 7, or 10 years), then it adjusts with the market. Great if you'll sell or refinance before the adjustment, risky if you stay.

If you want the security of a fixed payment, skip the ARM unless you're confident you'll move in the first few years. Compare both sides with our Fixed vs Adjustable guide.

When to buy points — and when not to

Discount points are prepaid interest: each point costs 1% of the loan amount and typically lowers your rate by about 0.25%. It only pays off if you stay in the home long enough to recoup the upfront cost.

A quick rule of thumb: if you plan to stay 5+ years, points can be worth it; if you'll likely move sooner, skip them and keep the cash.

How to get 3 quotes that actually compare

  1. Go to 3–4 lenders in the same week — rates move daily.
  2. Give them the same loan amount, term, and down payment so quotes are apples-to-apples.
  3. Compare APR, not just the rate — APR includes fees (see What Is APR).
  4. Ask about points and closing costs in each quote, not just the headline rate.
  5. Use the lowest written quote to negotiate with the others — lenders often match.

Common questions

What is a good mortgage rate right now?▾
It depends on your credit, loan type, and the market. As a rough benchmark, 30-year fixed rates typically fall in the 6% to 7% range, and 15-year fixed usually runs about 0.5 to 0.75% lower. Compare quotes from several lenders.
Why is my rate higher than the advertised rate?▾
Advertised rates are for the most qualified borrowers. Your actual rate depends on credit score, down payment, loan type, and loan size — plus whether you buy points.
How can I get a lower mortgage rate?▾
Shop multiple lenders, improve your credit score, make a larger down payment, or choose a shorter term. You can also buy discount points to lower the rate.
Should I lock my rate?▾
If rates are stable or rising, locking protects you. A rate lock typically lasts 30 to 60 days. Your lender can tell you the lock options available.
